Abba Bail Bonds Case Study With 11,665 Tracked Callers

The Problem

We audit a lot of bail bond sites. Most of them have the same hole. The phone rings, and nobody knows why it rang.

Abba Bail Bonds had a working office, real bondsmen and decades in the trade. What it did not have was a way to see which search, which county or which ad made a family pick up the phone. In bail, the first trusted number that answers gets the bond. Guessing is expensive.

What We Did

  • We put a tracking number on every channel so each call carried its source with it.
  • We split calls by county, because each county jail has its own intake and its own search habits.
  • We built county pages with their own call flow, then ran search work and paid ads side by side.
  • We routed calls that came from jail lookups into their own bucket so the office could count them.

The Measured Result

From December 2021 to January 2024, the call tracking counted 11,665 first time callers. These are people who dialed, not ad clicks or page views.

The jail call bucket alone logged 3,890 conversions from August 2022 through April 2024.

On the Los Angeles line we compared the same 28 days a year apart. January 1 to 28, 2023 brought 181 first time callers. The same days in 2024 brought 215. That is a 19 percent lift.

Monthly callers went from about 300 in January 2022 to about 550 to 600 by late 2023. Same office, same phones.
